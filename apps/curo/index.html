Time you can see.

Curo is a focus timer built around the parts of working that ADHD actually makes hard — knowing how much time has gone, facing one step instead of a list, and having somewhere to put the thought that just interrupted you.

What it does

Six things, each aimed at a specific failure mode.

⏱ TIME YOU CAN SEE

Already on screen

The countdown lives in the Mac menu bar and on the iPhone Lock Screen, so it's there when you glance — you never have to go and look for it. The digits sit inside a ring that empties, because a shape that's visibly two-thirds gone tells you where you are without doing arithmetic about it.

↗ RUNNING LONG

Overtime isn't failure

When the planned time is up the timer keeps counting — +3:12 — instead of stopping. In amber, never red. Sometimes the right move is to keep going, and an app can't know which time this is.

▸ ONE STEP

Not a list

Type a rough breakdown, one line each, and Curo shows exactly one of them. The rest are kept and hidden. A step you can't face goes to the back with "Later" — without being deleted or lied about.

⌥⌘␣ CAPTURE

Somewhere to put the thought

Press ⌥⌘Space anywhere on the Mac, or ask Siri on iPhone, and a one-line field takes whatever just interrupted you. It's out of your head in two seconds, and the queue waits on its own screen until you go looking — not while you're working.

▤ WHAT YOU DID

An honest record

A streak that survives an empty today and only breaks after a whole day passes. A fortnight's chart that draws the blank days instead of quietly closing the gaps. Sessions logged with focused time and wall-clock time side by side: 38 min focused across 71 min at the desk.

✓ NEVER LOSES TIME

Survives sleep and quit

Elapsed time is read from timestamps, not counted up by a ticking clock, so a session survives the Mac sleeping, the phone locking, or the app being killed. Quit mid-session, reopen an hour later, and the clock has moved on exactly as it should have.

What it doesn't do
