Foreground service use

Moderno Terminal declares Android’s FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE permission. This page explains what it is for and shows it working, for anyone reviewing the app.

What the recording shows

1 · CONNECT

A real SSH session

The app connects to a server and opens an interactive shell with a PTY. A long-running command is started — here, output streaming a line at a time.

2 · LEAVE

The app goes to the background

Home is pressed while the command is still running. The notification shade shows the ongoing notification, “SSH session connected — tap to return to your terminal”, naming what is still active.

3 · RETURN

The work continued

On return the output has advanced well past where it was — roughly 150 further lines arrived while the app was away. The session was never interrupted, restarted or replayed.

Why the permission is necessary. Without a foreground service, Android’s cached-app freezer suspends the process shortly after it leaves the screen and the SSH connection drops, killing whatever was running — a build, a backup, a database migration.

Why the task cannot be paused or restarted. An SSH session is stateful. The remote shell, its working directory, its environment and any process it started exist only for the life of that one connection. Reconnecting opens a new session on the server; the work in progress is gone. There is nothing to resume.

Scope. The service runs only while a session is open, shows an ongoing notification naming the connected host for the whole time, and stops the moment the session closes.
